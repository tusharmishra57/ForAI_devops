Title: Attention Is All You Need (Transformer Paper)
Authors: Ashish Vaswani, Noam Shazeer, Niki Parmar, Jakob Uszkoreit, Llion Jones, Aidan N. Gomez, Lukasz Kaiser, Illia Polosukhin

1. Introduction and Architectural Motivation
The dominant sequence transduction models were based on recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and LSTMs. The Transformer eschews recurrence and relies entirely on self-attention mechanisms to model global dependencies between input and output tokens. Removing recurrence enables significantly higher parallelization during training and solves the vanishing gradient problem over long sequences.

2. Model Architecture
The Transformer employs an encoder-decoder architecture:
- Encoder: A stack of N = 6 identical layers. Each layer contains two sub-layers: multi-head self-attention and a position-wise fully connected feed-forward network. Residual connections follow each sub-layer: LayerNorm(x + Sublayer(x)). Output dimension is d_model = 512.
- Decoder: A stack of N = 6 identical layers. Along with the two sub-layers in each encoder layer, the decoder adds a third sub-layer performing multi-head cross-attention over the encoder output. Autoregressive masking is applied in decoder self-attention to prevent positions from attending to future tokens.

3. Scaled Dot-Product Attention and Multi-Head Attention
Attention is computed using Queries (Q), Keys (K), and Values (V):
Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax((Q * K^T) / sqrt(d_k)) * V
Scaling by 1/sqrt(d_k) prevents the dot products from growing excessively large for large dimensions, which would cause vanishing gradients in softmax.
Multi-Head Attention projects Q, K, and V into h = 8 representation subspaces, allowing the model to simultaneously attend to information from different representation spaces:
MultiHead(Q, K, V) = Concat(head_1, ..., head_h) * W^O.

4. Positional Encodings
Because attention is permutation-invariant and has no inherent sense of sequence order, positional encodings are added to input embeddings:
PE(pos, 2i) = sin(pos / 10000^(2i/d_model))
PE(pos, 2i+1) = cos(pos / 10000^(2i/d_model))
